FUNCTIONS 10

Trigonometric Functions

Trigonometric functions describe angles and repeating behaviour.

The main idea

\[\sin x,\qquad\cos x,\qquad\tan x.\]

1. Sine, cosine and tangent

In a right triangle, sine, cosine and tangent are ratios of side lengths relative to an angle.

2. The unit circle

A point on the unit circle has coordinates \((\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\). The diagram connects the geometry directly to the two functions.

3. Radians

Calculus normally uses radians: one full revolution is \(2\pi\), and \(180^\circ=\pi\).

4. Periodic behaviour

Sine and cosine repeat every \(2\pi\). Their graph makes the repeating cycle and phase difference visible.

5. Values of sine and cosine

Important values include \(\sin0=0\), \(\cos0=1\), \(\sin(\pi/2)=1\), and \(\cos(\pi/2)=0\).

6. Tangent

Since \(\tan x=\sin x/\cos x\), tangent is undefined where \(\cos x=0\). The dashed vertical lines show these asymptotes.

7. A fundamental identity

\[\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1.\]

This follows directly from the unit circle equation.

8. Transforming trigonometric functions

In \(y=A\sin(Bx+C)+D\), \(A\) changes amplitude and \(B\) changes period. The comparison graph makes both effects visible.

9. Why trigonometric functions matter for calculus

\[\frac{d}{dx}\sin x=\cos x,\qquad\frac{d}{dx}\cos x=-\sin x.\]
