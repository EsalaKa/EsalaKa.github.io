MATHEMATICAL ANALYSIS 06

Extreme Value Theorem

Continuity on a closed bounded interval guarantees that maximum and minimum values are actually attained.

The theorem

If \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\), then there exist points \(c,d\in[a,b]\) such that

\[f(c)\le f(x)\le f(d)\qquad\text{for every }x\in[a,b].\]

Thus \(f(c)\) is an absolute minimum and \(f(d)\) an absolute maximum.

Why the hypotheses matter

On an open interval, an extreme value may be approached without being reached. A discontinuity can also destroy attainment.

Connection to optimisation

For a differentiable function, interior extrema are sought among critical points where \(f'(x)=0\), but endpoints must also be checked. The EVT explains why a global maximum and minimum exist under its hypotheses.
