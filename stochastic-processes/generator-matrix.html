STOCHASTIC PROCESSES 11

Generator Matrices

The generator matrix stores the transition rates of a finite-state continuous-time Markov chain.

The generator

Using a row convention, for \(i\ne j\),

\[q_{ij}\ge0\]

is the rate from state \(i\) to state \(j\). The diagonal is defined by

\[\boxed{q_{ii}=-\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij}}\]

so every row sums to zero.

1. Example

\[Q=\begin{pmatrix}-\lambda&\lambda\\ \mu&-\mu\end{pmatrix}.\]

The off-diagonal entries are transition rates; the negative diagonal records the total rate of leaving each state.

2. Why the diagonal is negative

For a small time \(h\),

\[P(h)\approx I+hQ.\]

The diagonal entry becomes

\[1+q_{ii}h=1-a_i h,\]

which is the approximate probability of remaining in state \(i\).

3. Generator versus transition matrix

A DTMC transition matrix contains probabilities and rows sum to one. A CTMC generator contains rates and rows sum to zero.

4. Transition probabilities through time

For a time-homogeneous finite CTMC,

\[\boxed{P(t)=e^{tQ}}\]

where the matrix exponential propagates transition probabilities through continuous time.

5. Forward equation

With row probability vectors,

\[\frac{d\pi(t)}{dt}=\pi(t)Q.\]

This is a Kolmogorov forward equation.

6. Central comparison

\[\boxed{\text{DTMC: }P\text{ contains probabilities}\qquad\text{CTMC: }Q\text{ contains rates}}\]
