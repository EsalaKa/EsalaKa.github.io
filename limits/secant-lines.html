CHANGE AND LIMITS 04

Secant Lines

A secant line joins two points on a curve and turns finite change into a measurable slope.

The main idea

For two distinct points

\[P=(x_1,f(x_1)),\qquad Q=(x_2,f(x_2)),\]

the straight line through them is the secant line. Its slope is

\[\boxed{m_{\rm sec}=\frac{f(x_2)-f(x_1)}{x_2-x_1}=\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}}.\]

1. A secant cuts the curve at two points

Unlike a tangent, a secant is defined using two distinct points. On \(y=x^2\), take

\[P=(1,1),\qquad Q=(3,9).\]

The straight line through \(P\) and \(Q\) is the secant.

2. The slope triangle

Moving from \(P\) to \(Q\) gives

\[\Delta x=x_2-x_1,\qquad\Delta y=f(x_2)-f(x_1).\]

These form the horizontal and vertical sides of the slope triangle.

3. Secant slope is average rate of change

For \(P=(1,1)\) and \(Q=(3,9)\),

\[\Delta x=2,\qquad\Delta y=8,\]

so

\[\boxed{m_{\rm sec}=\frac82=4}.\]

The same number is the average rate of change of \(x^2\) over \([1,3]\).

4. The secant depends on the second point

Keep \(P=(1,1)\) fixed. If \(Q=(2,4)\), the secant slope is \(3\). If \(Q=(3,9)\), it is \(4\).

Different choices of \(Q\) therefore produce different secant lines.

5. Move \(Q\) toward \(P\)

Write the second input as \(x+\Delta x\). Then

\[Q=(x+\Delta x,f(x+\Delta x)).\]

As \(\Delta x\) becomes smaller, \(Q\) moves closer to \(P\) along the curve.

6. Secants approach a tangent

As \(Q\to P\), the secant lines approach a limiting position: the tangent line at \(P\).

\[\text{secant slope}\longrightarrow\text{tangent slope}.\]

For \(y=x^2\) at \(x=1\), the limiting slope is \(2\).

7. Why \(\Delta x\) cannot simply equal zero

The secant slope is

\[\frac{f(x+\Delta x)-f(x)}{\Delta x}.\]

For a secant, \(\Delta x\ne0\). Substituting \(\Delta x=0\) immediately would divide by zero.

8. The transition to limits

The essential calculus transition is

\[\boxed{\text{two points}\to\text{secant}\to\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}\to\text{limit}\to\text{tangent}}.\]

Key takeaways

A secant line joins two distinct points on a curve. Its slope \(\Delta y/\Delta x\) is the average rate of change over that interval. When one point moves toward the other, the secant slope can approach the tangent slope, which motivates the mathematical idea of a limit.
