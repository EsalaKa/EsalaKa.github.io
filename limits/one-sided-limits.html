CHANGE AND LIMITS 08

One-Sided Limits

A one-sided limit studies what a function approaches from only one side of a point.

The main idea

A function may behave differently on the two sides of the same input value.

So we sometimes study the left and right sides separately.

1. Approaching from the left

The notation

\[ \lim_{x\to a^-}f(x)=L \]

means that \(f(x)\) approaches \(L\) as \(x\) approaches \(a\) using values smaller than \(a\).

For example,

\[ x=1.9,\quad 1.99,\quad 1.999,\ldots \]

approaches \(2\) from the left.

2. Approaching from the right

The notation

\[ \lim_{x\to a^+}f(x)=L \]

means that \(f(x)\) approaches \(L\) as \(x\) approaches \(a\) using values greater than \(a\).

For example,

\[ x=2.1,\quad 2.01,\quad 2.001,\ldots \]

approaches \(2\) from the right.

3. Why the signs are written above \(a\)

The symbols

\[ a^- \qquad\text{and}\qquad a^+ \]

do not mean negative \(a\) and positive \(a\).

They indicate the direction of approach:

\[ a^- \; \text{means from values below } a, \] \[ a^+ \; \text{means from values above } a. \]

4. The two-sided limit

The ordinary limit

\[ \lim_{x\to a}f(x) \]

exists only when both one-sided limits exist and are equal.

\[ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to a^-}f(x) = \lim_{x\to a^+}f(x) = L } \]

Then

\[ \lim_{x\to a}f(x)=L. \]

5. Example where the two-sided limit exists

Consider

\[ f(x)=x^2. \]

As \(x\) approaches \(2\) from the left,

\[ \lim_{x\to2^-}x^2=4. \]

From the right,

\[ \lim_{x\to2^+}x^2=4. \]

Therefore,

\[ \lim_{x\to2}x^2=4. \]

6. Example where the two-sided limit does not exist

Suppose

\[ f(x)= \begin{cases} 1, & x<0,\\ 3, & x\ge0. \end{cases} \]

Approaching \(0\) from the left gives

\[ \lim_{x\to0^-}f(x)=1. \]

Approaching \(0\) from the right gives

\[ \lim_{x\to0^+}f(x)=3. \]

Since the two sides approach different values,

\[ \boxed{ \lim_{x\to0}f(x) \text{ does not exist.} } \]

7. Visualising the jump

From the left, the graph approaches height \(1\). From the right, it approaches height \(3\).

Because these values are different, there is no single two-sided limit.

8. The value at the point is separate

In the previous example,

\[ f(0)=3. \]

But this does not change the left-hand limit:

\[ \lim_{x\to0^-}f(x)=1. \]

The value of the function at the point and the values approached from either side are separate ideas.

9. One-sided limits at boundaries

Sometimes only one direction of approach is possible.

For example, consider

\[ f(x)=\sqrt{x}. \]

Its real domain is

\[ x\ge0. \]

At \(x=0\), there are no allowed real inputs to the left. So the relevant limit is

\[ \lim_{x\to0^+}\sqrt{x}=0. \]

10. Why one-sided limits matter

One-sided limits help us understand jumps, boundaries and discontinuities.

\[ \text{left behaviour} \quad+\quad \text{right behaviour} \quad\longrightarrow\quad \text{two-sided limit} \]

They are also essential for understanding continuity.
