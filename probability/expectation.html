PROBABILITY 09

Expectation

Expectation is the probability-weighted average of a random variable and describes its mean value across repeated realisations.

Discrete expectation

For a discrete random variable,

\[\boxed{E[X]=\sum_x xP(X=x)}\]

1. Continuous expectation

For a continuous variable with density \(f_X\),

\[\boxed{E[X]=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x f_X(x)\,dx}\]

when the integral exists.

2. Expectation is not necessarily a possible outcome

A fair die has

\[E[X]=3.5,\]

although \(3.5\) cannot occur on a single roll. Expectation describes the distribution, not necessarily one realisation.

3. Functions of random variables

For a suitable function \(g\),

\[E[g(X)]=\sum_x g(x)P(X=x)\]

in the discrete case, or

\[E[g(X)]=\int g(x)f_X(x)\,dx\]

in the continuous case.

4. Linearity

\[\boxed{E[aX+bY+c]=aE[X]+bE[Y]+c}\]

Linearity does not require \(X\) and \(Y\) to be independent.

5. Products

If \(X\) and \(Y\) are independent and expectations exist,

\[E[XY]=E[X]E[Y].\]

This is not true in general without independence.

6. Expectation in stochastic modelling

If \(X(t)\) is a stochastic process, then \(E[X(t)]\) describes its mean state at each time. It does not describe the uncertainty around that mean.
