Quadratic Functions

A quadratic function contains a squared variable and has a curved graph called a parabola.

The main idea

\[f(x)=ax^2+bx+c,\qquad a\neq0.\]

1. Shape

If \(a>0\), the parabola opens upward. If \(a<0\), it opens downward.

2. Example

For \(f(x)=x^2\), values equally far from zero give the same output. The graph makes the symmetry visible.

3. Roots

Roots are where the graph meets the \(x\)-axis. For \((x-2)(x-3)\), the roots are \(2\) and \(3\).

4. Turning point

A parabola has one turning point: a minimum if it opens upward and a maximum if it opens downward.

5. Completing the square

The form \(a(x-h)^2+k\) reveals the turning point \((h,k)\). Thus \(x^2-4x+7=(x-2)^2+3\) has turning point \((2,3)\).

6. Axis of symmetry

For \(a(x-h)^2+k\), the axis of symmetry is \(x=h\).

7. Quadratic formula

\[x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}.\]

8. Discriminant

The graph shows the three geometric possibilities: two crossings, one touching point, or no real crossing of the \(x\)-axis.

9. Why quadratics matter for calculus

A quadratic has changing slope: \(f(x)=x^2\) gives \(f'(x)=2x\).
