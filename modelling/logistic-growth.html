CALCULUS FOR MODELLING 04

Logistic Growth

The logistic model reduces growth as a population approaches a limiting capacity.

The model

\[\boxed{\frac{dP}{dt}=rP\left(1-\frac{P}{K}\right)}\]

Here \(r\gt0\) is the intrinsic growth rate and \(K\gt0\) is the carrying capacity.

1. Why modify exponential growth?

The factor \(1-P/K\) reduces growth as \(P\) increases.

2. Small populations

If \(P\ll K\), then \(P'\approx rP\), so early logistic growth resembles exponential growth.

3. Near carrying capacity

When \(P\approx K\), growth slows toward zero.

4. Equilibria

\[\boxed{P^*=0\quad\text{or}\quad P^*=K}\]

5. Stability

For \(0\lt P\lt K\), \(P'\gt0\). For \(P\gt K\), \(P'\lt0\). Thus positive solutions tend toward \(K\).

6. Maximum growth rate

\[\boxed{P=\frac K2}.\]

7. Explicit solution

For \(P_0\gt0\),

\[\boxed{P(t)=\frac{K}{1+\left(\frac{K-P_0}{P_0}\right)e^{-rt}}}\]

8. Shape and interpretation

The solution is typically S-shaped below \(K\): near-exponential growth, fastest growth near \(K/2\), then flattening toward \(K\).

9. Limitation

Real populations may have changing resources, delays, seasonal effects or additional structure.
