CALCULUS FOR MODELLING 04

Logistic Growth

The logistic model modifies exponential growth by reducing the growth rate as a population approaches a limiting capacity.

The model

\[\boxed{\frac{dP}{dt}=rP\left(1-\frac{P}{K}\right)}\]

Here \(r>0\) is the intrinsic growth rate and \(K>0\) is the carrying capacity.

1. Why modify exponential growth?

The exponential model \(P'=rP\) assumes unlimited growth. The logistic factor

\[1-\frac{P}{K}\]

reduces growth as \(P\) increases.

2. Small populations

If \(P\ll K\), then \(P/K\) is small and

\[P'\approx rP.\]

Early logistic growth therefore resembles exponential growth.

3. Near carrying capacity

When \(P\approx K\), the factor \(1-P/K\) approaches zero, so growth slows.

4. Equilibria

Set \(P'=0\):

\[rP\left(1-\frac{P}{K}\right)=0.\]

Thus

\[\boxed{P^*=0\quad\text{or}\quad P^*=K}\]

5. Stability

For \(00\). For \(P>K\), \(P'<0\). Thus positive solutions tend toward \(K\).

6. Maximum growth rate

The growth term is largest at

\[\boxed{P=\frac K2}.\]

At this population size the absolute growth rate is \(rK/4\).

7. Explicit solution

With \(P(0)=P_0>0\),

\[\boxed{P(t)=\frac{K}{1+\left(\frac{K-P_0}{P_0}\right)e^{-rt}}}\]

8. Shape

For an initial population below \(K\), the solution typically has an S-shaped curve: approximately exponential at first, fastest near \(K/2\), then flattening toward \(K\).

9. Interpretation of \(K\)

Carrying capacity is a model parameter representing the population level supported under the assumptions of the model. It need not be a permanent physical constant.

10. Limitation

The logistic model compresses many environmental mechanisms into one density-dependent term. Real populations may have delays, seasonal forcing, age structure or changing resources.

11. The central idea

\[\boxed{\text{growth potential}\times\text{remaining capacity}}\]
