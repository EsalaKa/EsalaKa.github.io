MATHEMATICAL ANALYSIS 04

Continuity

A continuous function preserves limiting behaviour: nearby inputs produce nearby outputs.

Continuity at a point

\[\boxed{\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=f(a)}\]

This requires the function value to exist, the limit to exist, and the two to agree.

Epsilon-delta form

For every \(\varepsilon\gt0\), there is \(\delta\gt0\) such that

\[|x-a|\lt\delta\Longrightarrow |f(x)-f(a)|\lt\varepsilon.\]

Continuous on an interval

A function is continuous on an interval when it is continuous at every relevant point. Polynomials are continuous everywhere; rational functions are continuous wherever their denominators are nonzero.

Differentiability implies continuity

If \(f\) is differentiable at \(a\), then it is continuous at \(a\). The converse is false: \(|x|\) is continuous at \(0\) but not differentiable there.

Why continuity matters

The next major theorems require continuity. It prevents arbitrary jumps and allows global conclusions from local behaviour.
