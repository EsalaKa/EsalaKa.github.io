CALCULUS FOR MODELLING 08

Equilibria in Models

An equilibrium is a state at which the model predicts no instantaneous change.

The main idea

For an autonomous model

\[\frac{dX}{dt}=f(X),\]

an equilibrium \(X^*\) satisfies

\[\boxed{f(X^*)=0}\]

1. Why equilibrium matters

If the system starts exactly at an equilibrium, the model predicts that it remains there.

2. Exponential model

For \(X'=rX\), with \(r\ne0\),

\[rX^*=0\]

gives the equilibrium

\[\boxed{X^*=0}.\]

3. Logistic model

For

\[P'=rP\left(1-\frac{P}{K}\right),\]

the equilibria are

\[\boxed{P^*=0,\qquad P^*=K}.\]

4. Equilibrium does not mean no processes occur

In a compartment model, inflows and outflows may both be nonzero but balance exactly. Thus a constant state can result from opposing processes.

5. Systems of equations

For

\[X'=f(X,Y),\qquad Y'=g(X,Y),\]

an equilibrium \((X^*,Y^*)\) satisfies

\[\boxed{f(X^*,Y^*)=0,\qquad g(X^*,Y^*)=0}\]

6. Nullclines

The curves \(f(X,Y)=0\) and \(g(X,Y)=0\) are nullclines. Their intersections give equilibria.

7. Disease-free equilibrium

In epidemic modelling, an equilibrium with no infectious individuals is often called a disease-free equilibrium.

8. Endemic equilibrium

An equilibrium with a positive infectious level may be called endemic when the model supports persistent infection.

9. Finding versus understanding equilibria

Solving \(f=0\) locates equilibria. Stability analysis is then needed to determine what nearby trajectories do.

10. The central idea

\[\boxed{\text{equilibrium}=\text{state where all net rates of change vanish}}\]
