CALCULUS FOR MODELLING 09

Thresholds

A threshold is a critical value at which the qualitative behaviour predicted by a model changes.

The main idea

\[\boxed{\text{parameter below threshold}\;\longleftrightarrow\;\text{different behaviour}\;\longleftrightarrow\;\text{parameter above threshold}}\]

1. Growth threshold

For \(X'=rX\), the sign of \(r\) separates growth from decay:

\[r>0\Rightarrow\text{growth},\qquad r<0\Rightarrow\text{decay}.\]

The critical value is \(r=0\).

2. Epidemic threshold

For the basic SIR model,

\[R_0=\frac{\beta}{\gamma}.\]

The value

\[\boxed{R_0=1}\]

separates initial growth and decline in a fully susceptible population.

3. Why \(R_0=1\)?

Early in the simple SIR model,

\[I'\approx(\beta-\gamma)I=\gamma(R_0-1)I.\]

Therefore the sign of \(I'\) changes when \(R_0\) crosses 1.

4. Thresholds can involve the state

During an epidemic, infection grows when

\[\beta\frac{S}{N}>\gamma,\]

or equivalently

\[\frac{S}{N}>\frac{1}{R_0}.\]

5. Thresholds and interventions

An intervention can change parameters or states enough to cross a threshold, producing qualitatively different model behaviour.

6. Capacity thresholds

Some thresholds are external decision levels rather than dynamical boundaries. If \(C\) is hospital capacity, one may compare a predicted demand \(H(t)\) with \(C\).

7. Parameter uncertainty

If a threshold decision depends on an estimated parameter, uncertainty near the threshold can be important. A point estimate alone may hide this sensitivity.

8. Threshold versus equilibrium

An equilibrium is a state where rates vanish. A threshold is a critical value separating behaviours. They are related in many models but are not the same concept.

9. The central idea

\[\boxed{\text{threshold}=\text{boundary between qualitatively different model regimes}}\]
