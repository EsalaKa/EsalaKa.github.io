FUNCTIONS 09

Logarithmic Functions

A logarithm tells us the exponent needed to produce a given number.

The main idea

\[\log_bx=y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b^y=x.\]

A logarithm answers: to what power must \(b\) be raised to obtain \(x\)?

1. Example

Since \(2^3=8\), \(\log_2 8=3\).

2. Inverse relationship

Exponentials and logarithms undo one another.

3. Domain

For real logarithms, \(x\gt0\), with \(b\gt0\) and \(b\neq1\). Thus the domain is \((0,\infty)\).

4. Basic values

\[\log_b1=0,\qquad \log_bb=1.\]

5. Product law

\[\log_b(xy)=\log_bx+\log_by.\]

6. Quotient law

\[\log_b\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)=\log_bx-\log_by.\]

7. Power law

\[\log_b(x^r)=r\log_bx.\]

8. Natural logarithm

\[\ln x=\log_e x.\]

9. Inverse identities

\[\ln(e^x)=x,\qquad e^{\ln x}=x\quad(x\gt0).\]

10. Solving an exponential equation

If \(e^{2x}=7\), taking natural logarithms gives \(2x=\ln7\), hence \(x=\ln7/2\).

11. Why logarithms matter for calculus

\[\frac{d}{dx}\ln x=\frac1x,\qquad x\gt0.\]

Logarithms are fundamental in calculus, differential equations and modelling.
