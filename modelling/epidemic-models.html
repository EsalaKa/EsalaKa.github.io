CALCULUS FOR MODELLING 06

Epidemic Models

Compartmental epidemic models describe how individuals move between epidemiological states such as susceptible, infectious and recovered.

The SIR model

Divide a population of size \(N\) into

\[S(t),\qquad I(t),\qquad R(t),\]

with

\[S+I+R=N.\]

1. Infection flow

A common homogeneous-mixing assumption gives the infection rate

\[\boxed{\beta\frac{SI}{N}}\]

This transfers individuals from \(S\) to \(I\).

2. Recovery flow

If infectious individuals recover at per-capita rate \(\gamma\), the recovery flow is

\[\boxed{\gamma I}.\]

3. SIR equations

\[\boxed{\frac{dS}{dt}=-\beta\frac{SI}{N}}\]\[\boxed{\frac{dI}{dt}=\beta\frac{SI}{N}-\gamma I}\]\[\boxed{\frac{dR}{dt}=\gamma I}\]

4. Conservation

Adding the equations gives

\[\frac{d}{dt}(S+I+R)=0.\]

Thus the total population remains constant under these assumptions.

5. Interpreting \(dI/dt\)

The infectious population increases through new infections and decreases through recoveries:

\[\boxed{I'=\text{infection inflow}-\text{recovery outflow}}\]

6. Early epidemic growth

Initially, if most people are susceptible, the infectious equation is approximately

\[I'\approx(\beta-\gamma)I.\]

This shows why early epidemic growth can resemble exponential growth.

7. Basic reproduction number

For the simple SIR model in a fully susceptible population,

\[\boxed{R_0=\frac{\beta}{\gamma}}.\]

If \(R_0>1\), infection can initially grow; if \(R_0<1\), it tends to decline.

8. SEIR extension

Adding an exposed compartment \(E\) produces an SEIR model, allowing a latent period between infection and infectiousness.

9. Deterministic interpretation

These ODEs describe continuous population-level flows. They do not explicitly represent the randomness of individual infection and recovery events.

10. Assumptions matter

The basic model may assume homogeneous mixing, constant parameters and a closed population. More detailed models can relax these assumptions.

11. The central idea

\[\boxed{\text{epidemic transitions}\rightarrow\text{compartment flows}\rightarrow\text{coupled ODEs}}\]
