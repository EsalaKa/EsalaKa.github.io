CALCULUS FOR MODELLING 10

Stability in Models

Stability describes what happens when a model state is slightly disturbed from an equilibrium.

The main idea

For

\[X'=f(X),\]

suppose \(X^*\) satisfies \(f(X^*)=0\). We ask whether nearby trajectories return to \(X^*\) or move away.

1. Stable equilibrium

An equilibrium is locally attracting when nearby solutions move toward it.

\[\boxed{\text{small disturbance}\rightarrow\text{return toward equilibrium}}\]

2. Unstable equilibrium

If nearby solutions move away, the equilibrium is unstable.

3. Sign analysis

For a one-dimensional autonomous model, inspect the sign of \(f(X)\) on either side of an equilibrium. Positive \(f\) moves solutions right; negative \(f\) moves them left.

4. Derivative test

For a sufficiently smooth one-dimensional system, if

\[f'(X^*)<0,\]

the equilibrium is locally asymptotically stable. If

\[f'(X^*)>0,\]

it is unstable.

5. Logistic example

For

\[f(P)=rP\left(1-\frac{P}{K}\right),\]

we have

\[f'(P)=r-\frac{2rP}{K}.\]

Thus \(f'(0)=r>0\), so \(0\) is unstable for \(r>0\), while \(f'(K)=-r<0\), so \(K\) is stable.

6. Systems

For several variables, local stability is studied using the Jacobian matrix evaluated at an equilibrium.

7. Eigenvalues

For a linearised system, eigenvalues describe local directions and rates of change. If all eigenvalues have negative real parts, the equilibrium is locally asymptotically stable.

8. Stability and long-term behaviour

A stable equilibrium can explain why many different initial conditions produce similar long-term outcomes.

9. Local versus global stability

Local stability concerns sufficiently small disturbances. Global stability concerns a much larger set, possibly all admissible initial states.

10. The central idea

\[\boxed{\text{equilibrium tells where change stops; stability tells what happens nearby}}\]
