Exponential Functions

An exponential function changes by a constant factor over equal changes in the input.

The main idea

A basic exponential function has the form

\[\boxed{f(x)=Ab^x,\qquad A\ne0,\quad b>0,\quad b\ne1}.\]

The variable appears in the exponent. This is what makes exponential behaviour fundamentally different from polynomial behaviour.

1. Constant factor

For \(f(x)=2^x\), increasing \(x\) by one multiplies the output by \(2\):

\[1,\ 2,\ 4,\ 8,\ 16,\ldots\]

So

\[\frac{f(x+1)}{f(x)}=2.\]
\(x=0\to1\)
\(x=1\to2\)
\(x=2\to4\)
\(x=3\to8\)

each step multiplies by \(2\)

2. Exponential growth

If \(b>1\), then \(b^x\) increases as \(x\) increases.

For example, \(2^x\) grows slowly at first and then increasingly rapidly.

\(b>1\)
↓
constant multiplicative growth
↓
increasingly steep curve

5. Percentage growth and decay

If a quantity grows by proportion \(r\) each period,

\[\boxed{P_n=P_0(1+r)^n}.\]

For a \(5\%\) increase, the multiplier is \(1.05\). For a \(5\%\) decrease, it is \(0.95\).

increase by \(5\%\): multiply by \(1.05\)
decrease by \(5\%\): multiply by \(0.95\)

6. The number \(e\)

The constant

\[e\approx2.71828\]

arises naturally when change occurs continuously rather than in separate steps.

The function \(e^x\) is the most important exponential function in calculus.

discrete compounding
↓ increasingly frequent changes
continuous compounding
↓
\(e\)

7. Continuous exponential change

A common continuous model is

\[\boxed{P(t)=P_0e^{kt}}.\]

If \(k>0\), the quantity grows. If \(k<0\), it decays.

\(k>0\) → growth
\(k=0\) → constant
\(k<0\) → decay

8. Why \(e^x\) is special

The derivative of \(e^x\) is itself:

\[\boxed{\frac{d}{dx}e^x=e^x}.\]

So the instantaneous rate of change is equal to the current value.

value: \(e^x\)
rate of change: \(e^x\)

same function

9. Exponential models and differential equations

If a quantity changes at a rate proportional to its current amount,

\[\frac{dP}{dt}=kP,\]

then its solution is

\[P(t)=P_0e^{kt}.\]

This structure appears in population growth, radioactive decay, finance and many biological models.

current amount \(P\)
↓ determines
rate \(kP\)
↓ leads to
\(P_0e^{kt}\)

10. Linear versus exponential

Linear change adds a fixed amount, while exponential change multiplies by a fixed factor.

The graph compares \(4x+1\) and \(2^x\). The exponential function eventually grows much faster.

Key takeaways

Exponential functions are characterised by constant multiplicative change. The base \(b\) controls growth or decay, \(A\) gives the value at zero, and \(e^x\) is central to calculus because its derivative is itself.

Why this matters for calculus

Exponential functions provide one of the clearest links between a function and its rate of change. They are therefore fundamental in differential equations and models of continuous growth and decay.
