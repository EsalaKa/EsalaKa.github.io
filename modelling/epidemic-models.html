CALCULUS FOR MODELLING 06

Epidemic Models

Compartmental epidemic models describe movement between states such as susceptible, infectious and recovered.

The SIR model

\[S(t),\qquad I(t),\qquad R(t),\qquad S+I+R=N.\]

1. Infection and recovery flows

Infection flow:

\[\boxed{\beta\frac{SI}{N}}\]

Recovery flow:

\[\boxed{\gamma I}.\]

2. Equations

\[\boxed{\frac{dS}{dt}=-\beta\frac{SI}{N}}\]\[\boxed{\frac{dI}{dt}=\beta\frac{SI}{N}-\gamma I}\]\[\boxed{\frac{dR}{dt}=\gamma I}\]

3. Conservation

\[\frac{d}{dt}(S+I+R)=0.\]

4. Early epidemic growth

If most people are susceptible,

\[I'\approx(\beta-\gamma)I.\]

5. Basic reproduction number

\[\boxed{R_0=\frac{\beta}{\gamma}}\]

If \(R_0\gt1\), infection can initially grow; if \(R_0\lt1\), it tends to decline.

6. SEIR extension

An exposed compartment \(E\) can represent a latent period between infection and infectiousness.

7. Deterministic interpretation

The ODE model describes continuous population-level flows, not individual random events.

8. Assumptions

The basic model commonly assumes homogeneous mixing, constant parameters and a closed population.
