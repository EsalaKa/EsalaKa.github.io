CHANGE AND LIMITS 04

Secant Lines

A secant line joins two points on a curve. Its gradient gives the average rate of change between those points.

The main idea

Suppose two points on the graph of \(y=f(x)\) are

\[ P=(x_1,f(x_1)) \]

and

\[ Q=(x_2,f(x_2)). \]

The straight line through \(P\) and \(Q\) is called a secant line.

1. Horizontal change

Moving from \(P\) to \(Q\), the horizontal change is

\[ \Delta x=x_2-x_1. \]

2. Vertical change

The corresponding vertical change is

\[ \Delta y=f(x_2)-f(x_1). \]

3. Gradient of the secant line

The gradient of any straight line is

\[ \frac{\text{vertical change}} {\text{horizontal change}}. \]

Therefore the gradient of the secant line is

\[ \boxed{ m_{\text{secant}} = \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} = \frac{f(x_2)-f(x_1)} {x_2-x_1} } \]

This is exactly the average rate of change of the function between the two points.

4. Example

Consider

\[ f(x)=x^2. \]

Take

\[ P=(1,1) \]

and

\[ Q=(3,9). \]

Then

\[ \Delta x=3-1=2 \]

and

\[ \Delta y=9-1=8. \]

Therefore,

\[ m_{\text{secant}} = \frac82 = 4. \]

5. Move \(Q\) closer to \(P\)

Now keep \(P\) fixed and move \(Q\) closer to \(P\) along the curve.

The horizontal separation

\[ \Delta x \]

becomes smaller.

\[ Q \longrightarrow P \qquad\text{as}\qquad \Delta x\longrightarrow0. \]

As this happens, the secant line changes its direction.

6. Secant line approaching a tangent line

As \(Q\) approaches \(P\), the secant line approaches a particular limiting position.

\[ \text{secant line} \quad\longrightarrow\quad \text{tangent line}. \]

The gradient of this limiting line gives the instantaneous rate of change at \(P\).

7. Why we do not simply set \(\Delta x=0\)

Using

\[ \frac{f(x+\Delta x)-f(x)} {\Delta x}, \]

we cannot substitute

\[ \Delta x=0, \]

because the denominator would be zero.

Instead, we allow \(\Delta x\) to become arbitrarily close to zero without requiring it to equal zero.

\[ \boxed{ \Delta x\rightarrow0 } \]

This distinction is the reason we need the mathematical idea of a limit.

8. The central transition

We can now see the path from elementary algebra to calculus:

\[ \text{two points} \longrightarrow \text{secant line} \longrightarrow \frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x} \] \[ \Delta x\rightarrow0 \] \[ \text{tangent line} \longrightarrow \text{instantaneous rate of change}. \]

The next step is to understand precisely what the notation \(\Delta x\rightarrow0\) means.
