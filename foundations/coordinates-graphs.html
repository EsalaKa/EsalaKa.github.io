FOUNDATIONS 07

Coordinates and Graphs

Graphs turn numerical relationships into shapes, allowing us to see patterns and change that may be difficult to notice from formulas alone.

Why graphs matter

A table can list values and an equation can describe a rule, but a graph lets us see the whole relationship at once: whether a quantity rises, falls, bends, reaches a maximum or changes rapidly.

\[\boxed{\text{equation}\rightarrow\text{points}\rightarrow\text{graph}\rightarrow\text{visual behaviour}}\]

This visual interpretation becomes central in calculus because derivatives describe slope and integrals describe accumulation or area.

1. The coordinate plane

The horizontal \(x\)-axis and vertical \(y\)-axis meet at the origin \((0,0)\). A point \((x,y)\) records horizontal and vertical position.

2. Ordered pairs

The order matters: \((2,5)\) means \(x=2,y=5\), while \((5,2)\) is a different point.

3. Positive and negative coordinates

The point \((-3,2)\) lies three units left and two units above the origin. Coordinates therefore turn numbers into positions.

4. A graph represents a relationship

For

\[y=2x+1,\]

every allowed value of \(x\) produces a corresponding \(y\). All such pairs form the graph. For example, \((0,1)\) and \((2,5)\) both lie on it.

5. Gradient

For a straight line,

\[\boxed{\text{gradient}=\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}}\]

It tells us how much \(y\) changes for each unit change in \(x\). A positive gradient rises; a negative gradient falls.

6. Curves

The graph \(y=x^2\) is curved. Unlike a straight line, its slope changes from point to point. This creates the central calculus question: how can we measure the slope at one particular point?

7. Graphs in applications

If the horizontal axis represents time, a graph can show population growth, temperature, speed, infection prevalence or financial value. Its shape gives information about how the system is changing.

8. Connection to calculus

\[\boxed{\text{graph}\rightarrow\text{slope}\rightarrow\text{rate of change}\rightarrow\text{derivative}}\]

Calculus gives precise mathematical tools for the behaviour that graphs allow us first to see intuitively.
