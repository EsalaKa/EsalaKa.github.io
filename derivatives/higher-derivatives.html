DIFFERENTIATION 12

Higher Derivatives

A function can be differentiated more than once. Each derivative describes how the previous one changes.

The main idea

If

\[ y=f(x), \]

the first derivative is

\[ f'(x). \]

Differentiate again to obtain the second derivative:

\[ \boxed{ f''(x) = \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} } \]

1. First derivative

The first derivative measures the rate of change of the original function.

For example, if

\[ y=x^3, \]

then

\[ \frac{dy}{dx}=3x^2. \]

2. Second derivative

Differentiate again:

\[ \frac{d}{dx}(3x^2)=6x. \]

So

\[ \boxed{ \frac{d^2y}{dx^2}=6x } \]

The second derivative measures how the first derivative changes.

3. Notation

The second derivative may be written as

\[ f''(x), \qquad y'', \qquad \frac{d^2y}{dx^2}. \]

These all mean the same thing.

4. Third and higher derivatives

Differentiating again gives the third derivative:

\[ f'''(x). \]

More generally, the \(n\)-th derivative may be written

\[ \boxed{ f^{(n)}(x) } \]

or

\[ \frac{d^n y}{dx^n}. \]

5. Example

Let

\[ y=x^4. \]

Then

\[ y'=4x^3, \] \[ y''=12x^2, \] \[ y'''=24x, \] \[ y^{(4)}=24, \] \[ y^{(5)}=0. \]

Once a polynomial has been differentiated enough times, its derivatives eventually become zero.

6. Physical meaning

Suppose \(s(t)\) is position.

Then

\[ \frac{ds}{dt} \]

is velocity.

The second derivative is

\[ \frac{d^2s}{dt^2}, \]

which is acceleration.

\[ \text{position} \rightarrow \text{velocity} \rightarrow \text{acceleration} \]

7. Example with motion

Suppose

\[ s(t)=t^3. \]

Then velocity is

\[ v(t)=\frac{ds}{dt}=3t^2. \]

Acceleration is

\[ a(t)=\frac{d^2s}{dt^2}=6t. \]

8. Second derivative and curvature behaviour

The second derivative tells us how the slope changes.

If

\[ f''(x)>0, \]

the slope is increasing.

If

\[ f''(x)<0, \]

the slope is decreasing.

This helps describe the shape of a graph.

9. Concavity

If

\[ f''(x)>0, \]

the graph is locally concave upward.

If

\[ f''(x)<0, \]

the graph is locally concave downward.

10. Example

Consider

\[ f(x)=x^2. \]

Then

\[ f'(x)=2x \]

and

\[ f''(x)=2. \]

Since

\[ f''(x)>0 \]

everywhere, the parabola is concave upward everywhere.

11. Points of inflection

A point of inflection is a point where the concavity changes.

For example, consider

\[ f(x)=x^3. \]

Then

\[ f''(x)=6x. \]

For \(x<0\),

\[ f''(x)<0, \]

while for \(x>0\),

\[ f''(x)>0. \]

The concavity changes at

\[ x=0. \]

So \(x=0\) is an inflection point.

12. Why higher derivatives matter

Higher derivatives describe successive levels of change.

\[ f \rightarrow f' \rightarrow f'' \rightarrow f''' \rightarrow\cdots \]

They are important in motion, optimisation, differential equations and mathematical modelling.
