CALCULUS FOR MODELLING 03

Growth and Decay

Exponential models arise when the instantaneous rate of change is proportional to the current amount.

The main idea

\[\boxed{\frac{dX}{dt}=rX}\]

The rate is proportional to \(X\) itself.

1. Growth

If \(r>0\), then \(dX/dt>0\) whenever \(X>0\), so the quantity grows.

2. Decay

If \(r<0\), the rate is negative for positive \(X\), so the quantity decreases.

3. Solving the equation

Separate variables:

\[\frac{dX}{X}=r\,dt.\]

Integrating gives

\[\ln|X|=rt+C.\]

Hence

\[\boxed{X(t)=X_0e^{rt}}\]

4. Why exponential?

The exponential function is special because its derivative is proportional to itself:

\[\frac{d}{dt}e^{rt}=re^{rt}.\]

5. Relative growth rate

Dividing the model by \(X\) gives

\[\boxed{\frac{1}{X}\frac{dX}{dt}=r}.\]

Thus \(r\) is the instantaneous fractional growth rate.

6. Doubling time

For \(r>0\), the doubling time satisfies \(e^{rT_2}=2\), so

\[\boxed{T_2=\frac{\ln2}{r}}.\]

7. Half-life

For decay with rate constant \(k>0\), write \(dX/dt=-kX\). The half-life is

\[\boxed{T_{1/2}=\frac{\ln2}{k}}.\]

8. Applications

This model can describe unrestricted population growth, radioactive decay, simple elimination processes, compound growth and early-stage epidemic growth under restrictive assumptions.

9. Limitation

Exponential growth cannot continue indefinitely in most real systems because resources, competition or behaviour eventually change the rate.

10. The central idea

\[\boxed{\text{rate proportional to amount}\Longrightarrow\text{exponential change}}\]
