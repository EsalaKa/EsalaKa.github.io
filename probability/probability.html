PROBABILITY 03

Probability

Probability assigns a number between zero and one to an event and measures how likely that event is under a specified model.

The probability scale

\[\boxed{0\le P(A)\le1}\]

A probability of \(0\) represents impossibility in the model; probability \(1\) represents certainty.

1. Equally likely finite outcomes

If \(n\) outcomes are equally likely and \(m\) belong to event \(A\), then

\[P(A)=\frac{m}{n}.\]

2. Example

For a fair die, the event \(A=\{2,4,6\}\) has

\[P(A)=\frac36=\frac12.\]

3. Addition rule

For any two events,

\[\boxed{P(A\cup B)=P(A)+P(B)-P(A\cap B)}\]

If the events are disjoint, the intersection term is zero.

4. Complement rule

\[\boxed{P(A^c)=1-P(A)}.\]

5. Relative frequency

When an experiment is repeated many times, the observed relative frequency of an event often approaches its model probability:

\[\frac{\text{number of occurrences of }A}{\text{number of trials}}.\]

Probability is the theoretical quantity; relative frequency is an empirical estimate.

6. Probability depends on a model

A probability statement is meaningful only after the experiment, event and probability assumptions are specified.

7. Probability over a small time interval

If an event occurs continuously in time at rate \(\lambda\), then for sufficiently small \(\Delta t\),

\[P(\text{event in }\Delta t)\approx\lambda\Delta t.\]

The rate and probability are different quantities: the probability is approximately rate multiplied by interval length.
