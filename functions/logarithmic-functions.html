FUNCTIONS 09

Logarithmic Functions

A logarithm tells us the exponent needed to produce a given number.

The main idea

\[\log_bx=y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b^y=x.\]

1. Example

Since \(2^3=8\), \(\log_2 8=3\).

2. Inverse relationship

Exponentials and logarithms undo one another.

3. Domain

For real logarithms, \(x>0\). The graph shows why the logarithmic curve exists only to the right of the \(y\)-axis and approaches \(x=0\) without reaching it.

4. Basic values

\(\log_b1=0\) and \(\log_bb=1\).

5. Product law

\(\log_b(xy)=\log_bx+\log_by\).

6. Quotient law

\(\log_b(x/y)=\log_bx-\log_by\).

7. Power law

\(\log_b(x^r)=r\log_bx\).

8. Natural logarithm

\[\ln x=\log_e x.\]

9. Inverse identities

\(\ln(e^x)=x\) and \(e^{\ln x}=x\). The graphs of \(e^x\) and \(\ln x\) are reflections in \(y=x\).

10. Solving an exponential equation

If \(e^{2x}=7\), then \(x=\ln7/2\).

11. Why logarithms matter for calculus

\[\frac{d}{dx}\ln x=\frac1x.\]
