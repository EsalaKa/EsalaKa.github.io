CHANGE AND LIMITS 01

What Change Means

Change compares a final value with an initial value.

The main idea

If a quantity moves from one value to another, its change is

\[\boxed{\text{change}=\text{final value}-\text{initial value}}.\]

So change describes the difference between two states of the same quantity.

1. Positive change

If a quantity changes from \(4\) to \(9\), then

\[9-4=5.\]

The change is positive, so the quantity increased by \(5\).

initial value: \(4\)
↓
increase by \(5\)
↓
final value: \(9\)

2. Negative change

If a quantity changes from \(9\) to \(4\), then

\[4-9=-5.\]

The negative sign tells us that the quantity decreased by \(5\).

initial value: \(9\)
↓
change: \(-5\)
↓
final value: \(4\)

3. Change is not the final value

If a value changes from \(10\) to \(13\), then the final value is \(13\), but the change is

\[13-10=3.\]

The value and the change in that value are different quantities.

final value: \(13\)
≠
change: \(3\)

4. The symbol \(\Delta\)

A finite change is commonly written using the Greek capital letter \(\Delta\), read “delta”.

If \(x\) changes from \(x_1\) to \(x_2\), then

\[\boxed{\Delta x=x_2-x_1}.\]

Similarly,

\[\boxed{\Delta y=y_2-y_1}.\]
\(x_1\longrightarrow x_2\)
↓
\(\Delta x=x_2-x_1\)

\(y_1\longrightarrow y_2\)
↓
\(\Delta y=y_2-y_1\)

5. Change depends on two values

\(\Delta x\) is not another name for \(x\). It compares two values of \(x\).

For example, if

\[x_1=2,\qquad x_2=2.1,\]

then

\[\Delta x=2.1-2=0.1.\]
first value
\(x_1=2\)
↓ difference
\(0.1\)
↓
second value
\(x_2=2.1\)

6. Two quantities can change together

If \(y\) depends on \(x\), then changing \(x\) may also change \(y\).

\[x_1\to x_2,\qquad y_1\to y_2.\]

We can therefore compare \(\Delta x\) and \(\Delta y\).

\(x_1\to x_2\)
↓
\(\Delta x\)

\(y_1\to y_2\)
↓
\(\Delta y\)

7. From change to rate of change

Sometimes we want to know not only how much \(y\) changed, but how much it changed relative to the change in \(x\).

\[\boxed{\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}}.\]

This is the average rate of change over the interval.

change in output
\(\Delta y\)
÷
change in input
\(\Delta x\)
↓
average rate of change

8. Why this leads to calculus

Algebra compares two distinct points using the finite ratio

\[\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}.\]

Calculus asks what happens to this ratio as \(\Delta x\) becomes smaller and smaller.

\[\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}\longrightarrow\text{instantaneous rate of change}.\]
finite change
↓ smaller intervals
limit
↓
instantaneous change
↓
derivative
Key takeaways

Change means final value minus initial value. The symbol \(\Delta\) represents a finite change between two values. When two quantities change together, the ratio \(\Delta y/\Delta x\) measures average rate of change and leads naturally to the idea of a derivative.
