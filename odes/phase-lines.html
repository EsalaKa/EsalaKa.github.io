DIFFERENTIAL EQUATIONS 06

Phase Lines and Stability

A phase line shows how solutions of an autonomous differential equation move between equilibrium states.

The main idea

For \(y'=f(y)\),

\[\boxed{f(y)\gt0\Rightarrow y\text{ increases}}\]\[\boxed{f(y)\lt0\Rightarrow y\text{ decreases}}\]

1. Equilibria

Equilibria satisfy \(f(y)=0\).

2. Example

For \(y'=y(2-y)\), equilibria are \(0\) and \(2\).

For \(y\lt0\), \(y(2-y)\lt0\). For \(0\lt y\lt2\), it is positive. For \(y\gt2\), it is negative.

3. Stability

Arrows point toward \(y=2\), so it is stable. They point away from \(y=0\), so it is unstable.

4. Semi-stability

An equilibrium may attract from one side and repel from the other.

5. Derivative test

\[\boxed{f'(y^*)\lt0\Rightarrow\text{locally asymptotically stable}}\]\[\boxed{f'(y^*)\gt0\Rightarrow\text{unstable}}\]

If \(f'(y^*)=0\), inspect the signs directly.

6. Long-term behaviour

A phase line often reveals equilibria, stability and long-term direction without requiring an explicit solution.

7. Practical method

\[\boxed{f(y)=0\rightarrow\text{divide into intervals}\rightarrow\text{check signs}\rightarrow\text{draw arrows}\rightarrow\text{classify equilibria}}\]
