STOCHASTIC PROCESSES 15

Brownian Motion

Brownian motion is a continuous stochastic process whose increments are independent, normally distributed and scaled by elapsed time.

Definition

A standard Brownian motion \(\{W_t:t\ge0\}\) satisfies

\[W_0=0.\]

For \(0\le s

\[\boxed{W_t-W_s\sim N(0,t-s)}\]

1. Independent increments

Brownian increments over non-overlapping time intervals are independent.

2. Stationary increments

The distribution of \(W_{t+h}-W_t\) depends only on \(h\):

\[W_{t+h}-W_t\sim N(0,h).\]

3. Brownian increment

Over a short interval \(\Delta t\),

\[\Delta W=W(t+\Delta t)-W(t)\sim N(0,\Delta t).\]

Therefore we can generate it as

\[\boxed{\Delta W=\sqrt{\Delta t}\,Z,\qquad Z\sim N(0,1)}\]

4. Why the square root appears

If \(Z\) has variance \(1\), then

\[\operatorname{Var}(\sqrt{\Delta t}Z)=\Delta t.\]

Thus the standard size of a Brownian increment is proportional to \(\sqrt{\Delta t}\), not \(\Delta t\).

5. Continuous does not mean smooth

Brownian paths are continuous, but they are almost surely nowhere differentiable. Ordinary derivative-based calculus therefore cannot be applied to them in the usual way.

6. Mean and variance

\[E[W_t]=0,\qquad \operatorname{Var}(W_t)=t.\]

As time increases, the distribution spreads out.

7. From Brownian motion to stochastic calculus

Stochastic differential equations use infinitesimal Brownian change, written \(dW_t\), together with ordinary time change \(dt\):

\[dX_t=\mu(X_t,t)dt+\sigma(X_t,t)dW_t.\]
\[\boxed{\text{Brownian motion}\rightarrow dW_t\rightarrow\text{stochastic calculus}}\]
