STOCHASTIC PROCESSES 10

Transition Rates

A transition rate measures how quickly a continuous-time Markov process tends to make a particular jump.

Rate is not probability

A rate \(q_{ij}\) has units of inverse time. It is not restricted to the interval \([0,1]\).

For sufficiently small \(\Delta t\),

\[\boxed{P(i\to j\text{ during }\Delta t)\approx q_{ij}\Delta t}\]

The product is dimensionless and approximates a probability.

1. Infinitesimal definition

More precisely, for \(i\ne j\),

\[q_{ij}=\lim_{h\downarrow0}\frac{P(X_{t+h}=j\mid X_t=i)}{h}.\]

2. Total rate

If several transitions can leave state \(i\), the total exit rate is

\[a_i=\sum_{j\ne i}q_{ij}.\]

3. Small-time probabilities

\[P(\text{one particular jump})=q_{ij}h+o(h),\] \[P(\text{no jump})=1-a_i h+o(h).\]

The probability of two or more jumps is of smaller order than \(h\).

4. State-dependent rates

Rates can depend on the current state. In an epidemic model, an infection rate may be

\[b(i)=\beta\frac{(N-i)i}{N},\]

while a recovery rate may be

\[d(i)=\gamma i.\]

5. Competing events

When several event types are possible, their rates compete. The total rate controls when the next event occurs, while relative rates determine which event occurs.

\[\boxed{\text{rate}\times\text{small time}\approx\text{event probability}}\]
