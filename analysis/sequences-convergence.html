MATHEMATICAL ANALYSIS 02

Sequences and Convergence

A sequence converges when its terms eventually become arbitrarily close to one fixed number.

Sequences

A sequence is an ordered list \((a_n)\). For example, \(a_n=1/n\) gives \(1,1/2,1/3,\ldots\).

Convergence

\[\boxed{a_n\to L}\]

means: for every \(\varepsilon>0\), there is an integer \(N\) such that

\[n\ge N\quad\Longrightarrow\quad |a_n-L|<\varepsilon.\]

The tolerance \(\varepsilon\) may be as small as we wish; sufficiently late terms must lie inside it.

Example

For \(a_n=1/n\), choose \(N>1/\varepsilon\). Then \(n\ge N\) gives \(1/n<\varepsilon\), so \(1/n\to0\).

Divergence

A sequence that does not approach a finite limit diverges. For example \((-1)^n\) oscillates between \(-1\) and \(1\).

Useful theorem

Every convergent sequence is bounded. Also, every bounded monotone sequence converges.
