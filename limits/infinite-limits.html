CHANGE AND LIMITS 04

Infinite Limits

An infinite limit describes function values that grow without bound as the input approaches a finite value.

The main idea

For example,

\[\boxed{\lim_{x\to0}\frac1{x^2}=+\infty}.\]

This does not mean infinity is an ordinary function value. It describes unbounded behaviour near \(x=0\).

1. Growing without bound

As \(x\) gets closer to zero, \(1/x^2\) becomes larger: \(x=0.1\) gives \(100\), while \(x=0.01\) gives \(10000\).

2. Vertical asymptotes

If a function becomes unbounded as \(x\to a\), the line \(x=a\) is a vertical asymptote. For \(1/x^2\), the vertical asymptote is \(x=0\).

3. Positive and negative infinity

For \(1/x\),

\[\lim_{x\to0^+}\frac1x=+\infty,\qquad\lim_{x\to0^-}\frac1x=-\infty.\]

The two sides behave differently, so the two-sided limit is not a single infinite limit.

4. Example away from zero

For

\[f(x)=\frac1{(x-2)^2},\]

the denominator approaches zero as \(x\to2\), while remaining positive. Hence

\[\lim_{x\to2}\frac1{(x-2)^2}=+\infty.\]

5. Recognising the behaviour algebraically

When a denominator approaches zero, inspect its sign from each side. A very small positive denominator produces a large positive quotient; a very small negative denominator can produce a large negative quotient.

denominator → \(0^+\)
→ quotient may → \(+\infty\)

denominator → \(0^-\)
→ sign must be checked
Key takeaways

Infinite limits describe unbounded behaviour near a finite input. One-sided behaviour and signs matter, and vertical asymptotes are the graphical signature of this type of limit.
