FUNCTIONS 13

Transformations of Functions

A transformation changes the position or shape of a graph in a predictable way.

The main idea

Start with \(y=f(x)\). Changes inside or outside \(f\) produce related graphs.

1. Vertical translation

\(y=f(x)+a\) shifts a graph vertically. The comparison shows \(x^2\) and \(x^2+3\).

2. Horizontal translation

\(y=f(x-a)\) shifts a graph right by \(a\). Notice the apparently opposite sign inside the function.

3. Vertical stretching

\(y=af(x)\) multiplies every vertical distance from the \(x\)-axis by \(|a|\).

4. Vertical reflection

\(y=-f(x)\) reflects the graph in the \(x\)-axis. The graph compares \(x^2\) and \(-x^2\).

5. Horizontal reflection

\(y=f(-x)\) reflects the graph in the \(y\)-axis. For example, \(e^{-x}\) is the reflection of \(e^x\).

6. Horizontal scaling

\(y=f(bx)\) changes horizontal scale. Comparing \(\sin x\) with \(\sin(2x)\) makes the compression clear.

7. Several transformations together

\(y=2(x-3)^2+1\) shifts right by \(3\), stretches vertically by \(2\), and shifts upward by \(1\).

8. Inside and outside changes

Outside changes affect outputs and therefore act vertically; inside changes affect inputs and therefore act horizontally.

9. Why transformations matter

\[f(x)\longrightarrow A f\!\left(B(x-C)\right)+D.\]

Many apparently different graphs are transformed versions of a small number of basic functions.
