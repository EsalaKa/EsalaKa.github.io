FOUNDATIONS 08

Mathematical Change

Calculus is fundamentally about change, so before studying derivatives we need a precise way to measure how quantities move from one value to another.

Why change matters

Many quantities are interesting because they do not stay fixed: distance changes with time, temperature rises and falls, populations grow, and prices vary. Knowing a value tells us the state of a system; knowing its change tells us how the system is evolving.

\[\boxed{\text{change}=\text{final value}-\text{initial value}}\]

1. The symbol \(\Delta\)

The Greek capital letter \(\Delta\), read “delta”, represents a finite change. If \(x\) moves from \(x_1\) to \(x_2\),

\[\Delta x=x_2-x_1.\]

If \(x\) moves from \(3\) to \(8\), then \(\Delta x=5\).

2. Direction of change

If the final value is smaller, the change is negative. Moving from \(8\) to \(3\) gives

\[\Delta x=3-8=-5.\]

The magnitude tells us how much it changed; the sign tells us the direction.

3. Comparing two changes

If \(x\) and \(y\) both vary,

\[\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}\]

measures how much \(y\) changes for each unit change in \(x\). This is a rate of change.

4. Average rate of change

For \(y=f(x)\), between \(x_1\) and \(x_2\),

\[\boxed{\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}=\frac{f(x_2)-f(x_1)}{x_2-x_1}}\]

For \(f(x)=x^2\) between \(1\) and \(3\), the rate is \((9-1)/(3-1)=4\).

5. Why an average rate is not always enough

If a car travels 120 miles in 2 hours, its average speed is 60 mph, but it need not have travelled at 60 mph throughout the journey. Its speed may have changed continuously.

This distinction motivates calculus: we want to know the rate at a particular instant, not only over a whole interval.

6. From finite change to calculus

\(\Delta x\) is an actual finite interval. Calculus investigates what happens to the ratio of changes as that interval becomes arbitrarily small:

\[\boxed{\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}\longrightarrow\frac{dy}{dx}}\]

This transition from average change to instantaneous change is the central idea behind differentiation.
