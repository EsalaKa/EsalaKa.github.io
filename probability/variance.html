PROBABILITY 10

Variance and Standard Deviation

Variance measures the spread of a random variable around its expected value.

Variance

Let \(\mu=E[X]\). The variance is

\[\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(X)=E[(X-\mu)^2]}\]

Squaring prevents positive and negative deviations from cancelling.

1. Computational form

Expanding the square gives

\[\boxed{\operatorname{Var}(X)=E[X^2]-(E[X])^2}\]

This connects variance to the first and second moments.

2. Standard deviation

\[\boxed{\operatorname{SD}(X)=\sqrt{\operatorname{Var}(X)}}.\]

Standard deviation has the same physical units as \(X\), whereas variance has squared units.

3. Constant random variable

If \(X=c\) with certainty,

\[\operatorname{Var}(X)=0.\]

There is no uncertainty around the mean.

4. Scaling

For constants \(a,b\),

\[\operatorname{Var}(aX+b)=a^2\operatorname{Var}(X).\]

Adding a constant shifts the distribution without changing its spread.

5. Sum of independent variables

If \(X\) and \(Y\) are independent,

\[\operatorname{Var}(X+Y)=\operatorname{Var}(X)+\operatorname{Var}(Y).\]

Without independence a covariance term is required.

6. Mean versus uncertainty

\[\boxed{E[X]\;\text{describes centre},\qquad \operatorname{Var}(X)\;\text{describes spread}}\]

Two models can have the same expectation but very different variances and therefore very different risk.
