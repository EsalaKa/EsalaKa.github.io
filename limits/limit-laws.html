CHANGE AND LIMITS 07

Limit Laws

Limit laws let us calculate many limits from simpler limits.

The main idea

Suppose

\[ \lim_{x\to a}f(x)=L \]

and

\[ \lim_{x\to a}g(x)=M. \]

Then limits usually behave in the same way as ordinary algebraic operations.

1. Constant rule

The limit of a constant is the constant itself:

\[ \lim_{x\to a}c=c. \]

For example,

\[ \lim_{x\to3}7=7. \]

2. Sum rule

The limit of a sum is the sum of the limits:

\[ \lim_{x\to a}[f(x)+g(x)] = L+M. \]

For example,

\[ \lim_{x\to2}(x^2+x) = 4+2 = 6. \]

3. Difference rule

The limit of a difference is the difference of the limits:

\[ \lim_{x\to a}[f(x)-g(x)] = L-M. \]

4. Constant multiple rule

A constant multiplier can be taken outside the limit:

\[ \lim_{x\to a}[cf(x)] = cL. \]

For example,

\[ \lim_{x\to2}3x^2 = 3(4) = 12. \]

5. Product rule

The limit of a product is the product of the limits:

\[ \lim_{x\to a}[f(x)g(x)] = LM. \]

For example,

\[ \lim_{x\to2}x(x+1) = 2(3) = 6. \]

6. Quotient rule

The limit of a quotient is the quotient of the limits, provided the denominator does not approach zero:

\[ \lim_{x\to a} \frac{f(x)}{g(x)} = \frac{L}{M}, \qquad M\neq0. \]

For example,

\[ \lim_{x\to2} \frac{x+1}{x+3} = \frac35. \]

7. Power rule

For a positive integer \(n\),

\[ \lim_{x\to a}[f(x)]^n = L^n. \]

For example,

\[ \lim_{x\to3}x^2 = 3^2 = 9. \]

8. Direct substitution

For many familiar functions, the limit can be found simply by substituting the approaching value.

For example,

\[ \lim_{x\to2}(x^2+3x-1) \]

is

\[ 2^2+3(2)-1 = 9. \]

This works because polynomial functions are continuous.

9. When substitution fails

Consider

\[ \lim_{x\to1} \frac{x^2-1}{x-1}. \]

Direct substitution gives

\[ \frac00, \]

which is undefined.

This does not mean the limit does not exist. It means the expression must be simplified first.

Factorising,

\[ x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1). \]

So, for \(x\neq1\),

\[ \frac{x^2-1}{x-1}=x+1. \]

Therefore,

\[ \lim_{x\to1} \frac{x^2-1}{x-1} = \lim_{x\to1}(x+1) = 2. \]

10. Why \(0/0\) is important

An expression such as

\[ \frac00 \]

is called an indeterminate form.

It does not determine the limit by itself. Different functions can produce the form \(0/0\) but have different limits.

So when substitution gives \(0/0\), we need more analysis.

11. Example using several laws

Find

\[ \lim_{x\to2} \frac{x^2+3x+1}{x+4}. \]

Using the limit laws,

\[ \lim_{x\to2}(x^2+3x+1) = 4+6+1 = 11, \]

and

\[ \lim_{x\to2}(x+4)=6. \]

Therefore,

\[ \lim_{x\to2} \frac{x^2+3x+1}{x+4} = \frac{11}{6}. \]

12. The practical idea

When finding a limit, first try direct substitution.

\[ \boxed{ \text{Substitute first} \rightarrow \text{simplify if necessary} \rightarrow \text{then evaluate the limit} } \]

If substitution gives an ordinary number, the calculation is usually finished. If it gives an indeterminate form such as \(0/0\), further work is needed.
