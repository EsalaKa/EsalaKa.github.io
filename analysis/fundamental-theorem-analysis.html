MATHEMATICAL ANALYSIS 11

Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

The Fundamental Theorem explains why accumulation and differentiation are inverse operations.

Part I: differentiating accumulation

If \(f\) is continuous and

\[F(x)=\int_a^x f(t)\,dt,\]

then

\[\boxed{F'(x)=f(x)}\]

Increasing \(x\) by a small amount adds a thin region whose area is approximately \(f(x)\Delta x\), so the accumulated area changes at rate \(f(x)\).

Part II: evaluating definite integrals

If \(F'=f\), then

\[\boxed{\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=F(b)-F(a)}\]

Why this is profound

The definite integral is defined through accumulation, while the derivative is defined through local limits. The theorem proves that these apparently different constructions are connected.

Conditions matter

The standard elementary statement assumes continuity. More advanced analysis extends integration beyond continuous functions, but the hypotheses must always be stated.
