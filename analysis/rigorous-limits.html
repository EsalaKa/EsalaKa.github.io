MATHEMATICAL ANALYSIS 03

Limits More Rigorously

The epsilon-delta definition makes “approaches” mathematically precise.

The definition

\[\boxed{\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=L}\]

means that for every \(\varepsilon\gt0\), there exists \(\delta\gt0\) such that

\[0\lt|x-a|\lt\delta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad |f(x)-L|\lt\varepsilon.\]

What the symbols mean

\(\varepsilon\) specifies how close the output must be to \(L\). We choose \(\delta\) so that inputs sufficiently close to \(a\) force that output accuracy.

Example

For \(f(x)=2x\), to prove \(\lim_{x\to3}2x=6\), note

\[|2x-6|=2|x-3|.\]

Choosing \(\delta=\varepsilon/2\) makes \(|x-3|\lt\delta\) imply \(|2x-6|\lt\varepsilon\).

Why this matters

Derivatives, continuity and integrals ultimately depend on limits. The definition tells us exactly what those limiting statements mean.
