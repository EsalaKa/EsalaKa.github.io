Inequalities

Inequalities compare quantities when they are not necessarily equal.

The main idea

An inequality tells us that one quantity is larger or smaller than another.

\[ a

means that \(a\) is less than \(b\).

1. The main symbols

The four basic inequality symbols are

\[ <,\qquad >,\qquad \le,\qquad \ge. \]

For example,

\[ 3<5 \]

means \(3\) is less than \(5\), while

\[ x\le4 \]

means \(x\) may be less than or equal to \(4\).

2. Inequalities describe ranges of values

An equation often gives a particular value. An inequality usually describes many possible values.

For example,

\[ x>2 \]

includes

\[ 2.1,\qquad 3,\qquad 10, \]

and every other real number greater than \(2\).

3. Adding or subtracting

Adding or subtracting the same quantity from both sides preserves the inequality.

\[ a For example,

\[ x+3<8 \]

gives

\[ x<5. \]

4. Multiplying by a positive number

Multiplying both sides by the same positive number keeps the inequality direction unchanged.

\[ a0 \]

implies

\[ ac For example,

\[ \frac{x}{2}<3 \]

gives

\[ x<6. \]

5. Multiplying by a negative number

Multiplying or dividing by a negative number reverses the inequality sign.

\[ a-b. \]

For example,

\[ -2x<6. \]

Dividing by \(-2\) gives

\[ x>-3. \]

The sign reverses because multiplication by a negative number reverses order on the number line.

6. Double inequalities

Two inequalities can be written together.

\[ 2 means

\[ x>2 \]

and

\[ x<5. \]

So \(x\) lies between \(2\) and \(5\).

7. Interval notation

Ranges of real numbers can also be written using intervals.

\[ 2 can be written as

\[ (2,5). \]

If the endpoints are included,

\[ 2\le x\le5, \]

we write

\[ [2,5]. \]

Round brackets exclude an endpoint. Square brackets include it.
