Quadratic Functions

A quadratic function contains a squared term and produces a smooth U-shaped or inverted U-shaped curve called a parabola.

The main idea

A quadratic function has the form

\[\boxed{f(x)=ax^2+bx+c,\qquad a\ne0}.\]

Unlike a linear function, its rate of change is not constant. The slope changes continuously as we move along the curve.

1. The basic parabola

The simplest quadratic is

\[f(x)=x^2.\]

Its graph is symmetric about the \(y\)-axis. Opposite inputs have the same output: \(f(-2)=f(2)=4\).

2. The sign of \(a\) controls the direction

If \(a>0\), the parabola opens upward and its vertex is a minimum. If \(a<0\), the parabola opens downward and its vertex is a maximum.

3. The turning point

Every parabola has one turning point. It is the lowest point when the graph opens upward and the highest point when it opens downward.

For \(f(x)=x^2\), the turning point is \((0,0)\).

4. Roots are x-axis intersections

The roots of a quadratic are the values of \(x\) for which \(f(x)=0\).

For

\[f(x)=(x-2)(x-3),\]

the roots are \(x=2\) and \(x=3\).

5. Completing the square reveals the geometry

The form \(f(x)=a(x-h)^2+k\) shows the turning point directly as \((h,k)\).

For example, \(x^2-4x+7=(x-2)^2+3\), so the turning point is \((2,3)\).

6. The axis of symmetry

For \(f(x)=ax^2+bx+c\), the axis of symmetry is

\[\boxed{x=-\frac{b}{2a}}.\]

This is also the \(x\)-coordinate of the turning point.

7. The quadratic formula

For \(ax^2+bx+c=0\), the roots are

\[\boxed{x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}}.\]

The formula works whether or not the quadratic factorises easily.

8. The discriminant predicts the number of real roots

The quantity \(D=b^2-4ac\) determines how the parabola meets the \(x\)-axis.

\(D>0\): two real roots
\(D=0\): one repeated real root
\(D<0\): no real roots

9. Changing \(a\) changes the width

For \(y=ax^2\), larger \(|a|\) makes the parabola narrower; smaller \(|a|\) makes it wider.

10. Why the slope changes

A parabola is curved, so its gradient depends on position.

For \(f(x)=x^2\), the derivative is

\[f'(x)=2x.\]

The slope is negative for \(x<0\), zero at \(x=0\), and positive for \(x>0\).

Key takeaways

A quadratic function produces a smooth parabola. Its coefficient \(a\) controls direction and width, its turning point lies on the axis of symmetry, its roots are the \(x\)-axis intersections, and the discriminant determines how many real roots exist.

Why this matters for calculus

Quadratics are the simplest functions with continuously changing slope. They provide a natural first example of how differentiation describes increasing, decreasing and stationary behaviour.
