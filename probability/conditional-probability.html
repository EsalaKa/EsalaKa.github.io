PROBABILITY 04

Conditional Probability

Conditional probability describes probability after information is known to have occurred.

The main definition

If \(P(B)>0\),

\[\boxed{P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(A\cap B)}{P(B)}}\]

Read \(P(A\mid B)\) as “the probability of \(A\) given \(B\).”

1. Why the denominator changes

Once \(B\) is known, the possible outcomes are restricted to \(B\). Conditional probability measures how much of that restricted probability belongs to \(A\).

2. Multiplication rule

Rearranging the definition gives

\[\boxed{P(A\cap B)=P(A\mid B)P(B)}.\]

Equally,

\[P(A\cap B)=P(B\mid A)P(A).\]

3. Example

For a fair die, let \(B\) be “the result is even” and \(A\) be “the result is greater than 3.” Then

\[B=\{2,4,6\},\qquad A\cap B=\{4,6\},\]

so

\[P(A\mid B)=\frac{2/6}{3/6}=\frac23.\]

4. Bayes' theorem

Combining the two multiplication forms gives

\[\boxed{P(A\mid B)=\frac{P(B\mid A)P(A)}{P(B)}}\]

Bayes' theorem reverses the direction of conditioning.

5. Conditioning changes the probability space

\[\boxed{\text{new information}\rightarrow\text{restricted possibilities}\rightarrow\text{updated probability}}\]
