FUNCTIONS 09

Logarithmic Functions

A logarithm tells us the exponent needed to produce a given number.

The main idea

The logarithmic statement

\[ \log_b x=y \]

means exactly the same as

\[ b^y=x. \]

A logarithm therefore answers the question: to what power must the base \(b\) be raised to obtain \(x\)?

1. A simple example

Since

\[ 2^3=8, \]

we have

\[ \log_2 8=3. \]

The logarithm gives the exponent \(3\).

2. Logarithms reverse exponentials

Exponentiation takes an exponent and produces a value:

\[ 2^3=8. \]

A logarithm reverses this process:

\[ \log_2 8=3. \]

So exponential and logarithmic functions are inverse operations.

3. The domain of a logarithm

For real logarithms,

\[ \log_b x \]

is defined only when

\[ x>0, \]

with

\[ b>0, \qquad b\neq1. \]

Therefore the domain of a logarithmic function is

\[ (0,\infty). \]

4. Some basic logarithm values

Because

\[ b^0=1, \]

we have

\[ \log_b 1=0. \]

Because

\[ b^1=b, \]

we also have

\[ \log_b b=1. \]

5. The product law

Multiplication inside a logarithm becomes addition:

\[ \log_b(xy) = \log_b x+\log_b y. \]

This follows from the index law

\[ b^m b^n=b^{m+n}. \]

6. The quotient law

Division inside a logarithm becomes subtraction:

\[ \log_b\left(\frac{x}{y}\right) = \log_b x-\log_b y. \]

This corresponds to the exponent law

\[ \frac{b^m}{b^n}=b^{m-n}. \]

7. The power law

An exponent inside a logarithm can be brought in front:

\[ \log_b(x^r) = r\log_b x. \]

The logarithm laws are therefore closely connected to the laws of exponents.

8. The natural logarithm

The logarithm with base \(e\) is called the natural logarithm.

\[ \ln x=\log_e x. \]

For example,

\[ \ln e=1 \]

because

\[ e^1=e. \]

9. \(e^x\) and \(\ln x\) undo each other

Because the exponential and logarithmic functions are inverses,

\[ \ln(e^x)=x \]

and, for \(x>0\),

\[ e^{\ln x}=x. \]

This relationship is especially useful when solving exponential equations.

10. Solving an exponential equation

Suppose

\[ e^{2x}=7. \]

Taking the natural logarithm of both sides gives

\[ \ln(e^{2x})=\ln 7. \]

Therefore,

\[ 2x=\ln7, \]

and hence

\[ x=\frac{\ln7}{2}. \]

11. Why logarithms matter for calculus

The natural logarithm has the derivative

\[ \frac{d}{dx}\ln x=\frac{1}{x}, \qquad x>0. \]

Logarithms also allow exponential relationships to be rearranged and analysed more easily.

Together, \(e^x\) and \(\ln x\) appear throughout calculus, differential equations and mathematical modelling.
