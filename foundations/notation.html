FOUNDATIONS 09

Mathematical Notation

Mathematical notation is a precise language for representing quantities, operations and relationships.

The main idea

A mathematical symbol is a compact way of expressing an idea. Understanding the symbol means understanding what it represents.

\[ \text{symbol} \quad\longrightarrow\quad \text{mathematical meaning} \]

1. Variables and symbols

Letters commonly represent quantities:

\[ x,\qquad y,\qquad t,\qquad n. \]

The meaning of a symbol depends on how it is defined. For example, \(t\) often represents time, but this is a convention rather than a mathematical rule.

2. Equality and inequality

The symbol

\[ = \]

means that two expressions have the same value.

The symbols

\[ <,\qquad >,\qquad \le,\qquad \ge \]

describe the ordering of quantities.

3. Brackets

Brackets group parts of an expression and determine which operations belong together.

For example,

\[ 2(x+3) \]

means that \(2\) multiplies the entire expression \(x+3\).

This is different from

\[ 2x+3. \]

4. Superscripts

A superscript commonly represents a power.

\[ x^2 \]

is read as “\(x\) squared” and means

\[ x\times x. \]

Similarly,

\[ x^n \]

is read as “\(x\) to the power \(n\)”.

5. Subscripts

A subscript labels or distinguishes quantities.

\[ x_1,\qquad x_2,\qquad x_3. \]

These are read as “\(x\) sub one”, “\(x\) sub two” and “\(x\) sub three”.

A subscript is normally a label, not multiplication.

6. Fractions

The notation

\[ \frac{a}{b} \]

means \(a\) divided by \(b\), where \(b\neq0\).

The fraction bar also groups the numerator and denominator. For example,

\[ \frac{x+1}{x-1} \]

means the whole expression \(x+1\) is divided by the whole expression \(x-1\).

7. Sets and membership

Braces are commonly used to describe a set:

\[ A=\{1,2,3\}. \]

The symbol

\[ \in \]

means “is an element of”.

For example,

\[ 2\in A \]

means that \(2\) belongs to the set \(A\).

8. Subsets

The symbol

\[ \subset \]

describes containment between sets.

For example,

\[ \mathbb N\subset\mathbb R \]

means that the natural numbers are contained within the real numbers.

9. Greek letters

Greek letters are frequently used as mathematical symbols.

Examples include

\[ \alpha,\qquad \beta,\qquad \gamma,\qquad \Delta,\qquad \pi. \]

Their mathematical meaning comes from the context in which they are defined.

For example, \(\Delta\) is commonly used to represent a finite change:

\[ \Delta x=x_2-x_1. \]

10. Function notation

The notation

\[ f(x) \]

is read as “\(f\) of \(x\)”.

It means the value produced by the function \(f\) when its input is \(x\).

For example, if

\[ f(x)=x^2+1, \]

then

\[ f(3)=3^2+1=10. \]

The brackets in \(f(x)\) indicate a function input; they do not mean ordinary multiplication.

11. Different symbols can represent different kinds of change

As we move into calculus, notation becomes especially important.

For example,

\[ \Delta x \]

represents a finite change in \(x\), while later we will encounter

\[ dx \]

in differential notation.

These symbols should not be treated as interchangeable simply because both involve change. Their precise meanings will be developed when we reach calculus.

12. Read mathematics structurally

A mathematical expression should be read according to its structure, not simply from left to right.

For example,

\[ \frac{(x+1)^2}{3} \]

can be understood in stages:

\[ x \longrightarrow x+1 \longrightarrow (x+1)^2 \longrightarrow \frac{(x+1)^2}{3}. \]

Recognising this structure makes more advanced notation much easier to understand.
