CHANGE AND LIMITS 01

What Change Means

Calculus begins by comparing how a function changes between two nearby inputs.

The main idea

For a function \(y=f(x)\), if the input changes from \(x_1\) to \(x_2\), then

\[\boxed{\Delta x=x_2-x_1},\qquad\boxed{\Delta y=f(x_2)-f(x_1)}.\]

These two finite changes are the starting point for rates of change and, later, derivatives.

1. Change on a curve

Take \(f(x)=x^2\). Moving from \(x=1\) to \(x=3\) moves along the curve from \(A=(1,1)\) to \(B=(3,9)\).

\[\Delta x=3-1=2,\qquad \Delta y=9-1=8.\]

The graph shows these horizontal and vertical changes directly.

2. Input change and output change are different

\(\Delta x\) measures movement along the input axis. \(\Delta y\) measures the resulting change in the function value.

\[x_1\to x_2\quad\text{causes}\quad f(x_1)\to f(x_2).\]

They describe different quantities and need not have the same size.

4. Change depends on the interval

For \(f(x)=x^2\), starting at \(x=1\):

\[1\to2\quad\Rightarrow\quad\Delta y=4-1=3,\]\[1\to3\quad\Rightarrow\quad\Delta y=9-1=8.\]

The amount of change depends on which two points are compared.

5. Finite change means two distinct points

At this stage, \(x_1\) and \(x_2\) are distinct, so

\[\boxed{\Delta x\ne0}.\]

The interval may be large or extremely small, but it still has a non-zero width.

6. From change to rate

Change becomes more informative when output change is compared with input change:

\[\boxed{\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}}.\]

Geometrically, this is the slope of the secant line through the two points on the curve.

7. Why this leads to calculus

The central question is what happens to the secant slope when the second point moves closer and closer to the first.

\[\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}\quad\longrightarrow\quad\text{instantaneous rate of change}.\]

This transition from finite change to a limiting rate is the foundation of differentiation.

Key takeaways

For \(y=f(x)\), finite change is described by \(\Delta x=x_2-x_1\) and \(\Delta y=f(x_2)-f(x_1)\). Their ratio gives the secant slope, which becomes the starting point for the derivative when the interval is made smaller.
