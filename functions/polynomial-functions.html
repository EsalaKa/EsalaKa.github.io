FUNCTIONS 06

Polynomial Functions

A polynomial is built from powers of a variable with non-negative integer exponents.

The main idea

A polynomial function has the form

\[ f(x) = a_nx^n+a_{n-1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+a_1x+a_0, \]

where the exponents are non-negative integers and the coefficients are constants.

1. Examples

These are polynomial functions:

\[ f(x)=3x+2, \] \[ g(x)=x^2-4x+1, \] \[ h(x)=2x^4-x^3+5. \]

Linear and quadratic functions are therefore special cases of polynomial functions.

2. Degree

The degree of a non-zero polynomial is the highest power of the variable with a non-zero coefficient.

For example,

\[ f(x)=4x^5-2x^2+7 \]

has degree

\[ 5. \]

The degree gives useful information about the overall behaviour of the polynomial.

3. Coefficients

In

\[ f(x)=3x^3-5x+2, \]

the coefficients are

\[ 3,\qquad 0,\qquad -5,\qquad 2. \]

The coefficient of \(x^2\) is \(0\), even though that term is not written explicitly.

4. Constant term

The term with no variable is the constant term.

For

\[ f(x)=2x^3+x-6, \]

the constant term is

\[ -6. \]

It also gives the value of the polynomial at \(x=0\):

\[ f(0)=-6. \]

5. Roots

A root of a polynomial is a value of \(x\) for which

\[ f(x)=0. \]

For example,

\[ f(x)=x^2-5x+6 \]

can be written as

\[ f(x)=(x-2)(x-3). \]

Therefore the roots are

\[ x=2 \qquad\text{and}\qquad x=3. \]

6. Factors and roots

If \(x=r\) is a root of a polynomial, then

\[ x-r \]

is a factor.

So the statements

\[ f(r)=0 \]

and

\[ x-r \text{ is a factor of } f(x) \]

describe the same underlying idea.

7. End behaviour

For large values of \(|x|\), the highest-power term usually dominates the behaviour of the polynomial.

For example, in

\[ f(x)=2x^4-3x+1, \]

the term

\[ 2x^4 \]

has the strongest effect when \(|x|\) becomes very large.

8. Smoothness

Polynomial functions have no breaks, jumps or sharp corners.

Their graphs are smooth and continuous for all real \(x\).

This makes them especially important in calculus.

9. Why polynomial functions matter for calculus

Polynomials are among the easiest functions to differentiate and integrate.

For example,

\[ f(x)=x^3 \]

has derivative

\[ f'(x)=3x^2. \]

More complicated functions can also often be approximated by polynomials.
