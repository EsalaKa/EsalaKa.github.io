FUNCTIONS 10

Trigonometric Functions

Trigonometric functions connect angles, circular motion and repeating change.

The main idea

The three basic trigonometric functions are

\[\boxed{\sin x,\qquad\cos x,\qquad\tan x}.\]

They begin as ratios in triangles, but the unit circle extends them to all real inputs and reveals their periodic behaviour.

1. Right-triangle ratios

For an acute angle \(\theta\),

\[\sin\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{hypotenuse}},\quad \cos\theta=\frac{\text{adjacent}}{\text{hypotenuse}},\quad \tan\theta=\frac{\text{opposite}}{\text{adjacent}}.\]
opposite / hypotenuse → sine
adjacent / hypotenuse → cosine
opposite / adjacent → tangent

2. The unit circle

On the circle of radius \(1\), the point reached by angle \(\theta\) has coordinates

\[\boxed{(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)}.\]

This gives sine and cosine meaning for every angle, not only angles in a right triangle.

3. Radians

Calculus uses radians because they measure angle directly from arc length.

\[180^\circ=\pi\text{ radians},\qquad360^\circ=2\pi.\]

On a unit circle, an angle of \(\theta\) radians cuts off an arc of length \(\theta\).

half turn → \(\pi\)
full turn → \(2\pi\)
quarter turn → \(\pi/2\)

4. Sine and cosine graphs

Both functions repeat every \(2\pi\):

\[\sin(x+2\pi)=\sin x,\qquad\cos(x+2\pi)=\cos x.\]

Sine begins at \(0\); cosine begins at \(1\). Their shapes are the same but shifted horizontally.

5. Exact key values

The graph highlights values that should become familiar:

\[\sin0=0,\quad\sin\frac{\pi}{2}=1,\quad\sin\pi=0,\]\[\cos0=1,\quad\cos\frac{\pi}{2}=0,\quad\cos\pi=-1.\]

6. Tangent and its asymptotes

Because

\[\tan x=\frac{\sin x}{\cos x},\]

tangent is undefined whenever \(\cos x=0\). Thus vertical asymptotes occur at

\[x=\frac{\pi}{2}+k\pi.\]

7. The fundamental identity

From the unit-circle equation \(x^2+y^2=1\) and \((x,y)=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)\),

\[\boxed{\sin^2\theta+\cos^2\theta=1}.\]
\(x^2+y^2=1\)
\(x=\cos\theta,\ y=\sin\theta\)
↓
\(\cos^2\theta+\sin^2\theta=1\)

8. Amplitude and period

For

\[y=A\sin(Bx),\]

the amplitude is \(|A|\), while the period is

\[\boxed{\frac{2\pi}{|B|}}.\]

The graph compares \(\sin x\), \(2\sin x\), and \(\sin(2x)\).

9. Horizontal and vertical shifts

In

\[y=A\sin(Bx+C)+D,\]

\(C\) changes the horizontal position and \(D\) changes the vertical position.

For example, \(\sin(x-\pi/2)\) is \(\sin x\) shifted right by \(\pi/2\).

10. Why trigonometric functions matter for calculus

Sine and cosine are linked directly through differentiation:

\[\boxed{\frac{d}{dx}\sin x=\cos x},\qquad \boxed{\frac{d}{dx}\cos x=-\sin x}.\]

This makes them fundamental for modelling oscillation, waves and periodic change.

\(\sin x\xrightarrow{d/dx}\cos x\)
\(\cos x\xrightarrow{d/dx}-\sin x\)
Key takeaways

Sine and cosine come from coordinates on the unit circle and repeat every \(2\pi\). Tangent is their ratio and has vertical asymptotes. Amplitude controls height, period controls repetition, and radians are the natural angle measure for calculus.
