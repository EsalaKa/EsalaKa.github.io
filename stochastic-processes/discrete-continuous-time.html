STOCHASTIC PROCESSES 03

Discrete and Continuous Time

A stochastic process can be indexed at separate time steps or at every point in continuous time.

Discrete time

A discrete-time process is observed at times such as

\[t_0,t_1,t_2,\ldots\]

often with a fixed step \(\Delta t\):

\[t_n=n\Delta t.\]

We may write the process as \(X_0,X_1,X_2,\ldots\).

1. Continuous time

A continuous-time process is defined for every time in an interval:

\[\boxed{\{X_t:t\ge0\}}.\]

Events need not wait for a fixed observation step.

2. Time type and state type are different

A process can have discrete time and discrete states, continuous time and discrete states, or continuous time and continuous states. These are separate modelling choices.

3. DTMC

A discrete-time Markov chain changes or remains in its state at fixed steps. Transition probabilities describe what happens at the next step.

4. CTMC

A continuous-time Markov chain occupies discrete states but jumps at random continuous times. Transition rates describe how quickly jumps occur.

5. Brownian motion

Brownian motion has continuous time and a continuous state space. Its path changes continuously rather than through discrete jumps.

6. Why the distinction matters

\[\boxed{\text{discrete time}\rightarrow\text{step probabilities},\qquad \text{continuous time}\rightarrow\text{rates and waiting times}}\]
